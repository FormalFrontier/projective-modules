# Projective Modules

A reusable Lean library for projective and free modules, scalar extension,
exterior algebra, cancellation, local freeness, stable categories, right-module
K₀ and rank.
Its results have different commutativity, handedness and finiteness assumptions;
the declarations and focused modules below specify each interface. Maintained by
the Formal Frontier source-maintainer team.

## Headline results

- **Right modules over coefficient products:** For any finite family of possibly
  noncommutative rings, a right module over their product is reconstructed from
  its scalar extensions along the coordinate projections. Coordinate extension
  of an assembled family recovers its factor even for an infinite index type.
  [The coefficient-product interfaces](ProjectiveModules/Module/RightProduct.lean)
  specify named coordinatewise actions over semirings and preserve projectivity
  under finite products without finite-generation assumptions. Over rings they
  assemble finite-projective factors as canonical bundled modules. Binary scalar
  and carrier universes are independent; empty families and zero rings are
  included. Supported coordinate inclusions are linear data, not unital maps.
- **Projective classes and K₀ of product rings:** For any finite family of
  possibly noncommutative rings, projection scalar extension gives an additive
  monoid equivalence of finite-projective classes and an additive group
  equivalence of K₀ with the respective products of the factor objects.
  [The product equivalences](ProjectiveModules/KTheory/Product.lean) specify their
  whole forward homomorphisms and inverse module generators, compatibility with
  group completion, signed inverse formulas and coordinate equality detection.
  Binary factors and module carriers have independent universes; empty families
  and zero rings are included. No cancellation of classes or injectivity of the
  class-to-K₀ map is assumed.
- **Noetherian submodules:** Over a possibly noncommutative left-Noetherian
  ring, any submodule of a module spanned by a sequence is itself spanned by a
  sequence, without assuming that the ambient module is Noetherian, free,
  projective or finitely generated. The ring and module may inhabit independent
  universes. See
  [`Module.CountablyGenerated.submodule_of_isNoetherianRing`](ProjectiveModules/Module/CountablyGenerated/Noetherian.lean)
  and the [proof and example guide](docs/NoetherianSubmodule.md).
- **Freeness over a commutative PID:**
  [`Submodule.free_of_pid_of_free` and `Module.Projective.free_of_pid`](ProjectiveModules/Module/PID.lean)
  cover arbitrary-rank free ambient modules and arbitrary-rank projectives,
  respectively. They do not turn the preceding general Noetherian theorem into
  a freeness statement.
- **Idempotent ideals:** The
  [canonical left-ideal interface](ProjectiveModules/Module/LocallyConstantRank.lean)
  uses arbitrary semirings for the right-multiplication projection,
  retraction and projectivity, and arbitrary rings for the complementary-ideal
  product equivalence. No centrality or nontriviality is required. Over a
  commutative semiring, the ideal generated by an idempotent `e` is free
  exactly when `e = 0` or `e = 1`; this criterion requires no domain or local
  hypothesis. A proper nonzero commutative idempotent therefore gives a
  nonfree finite projective module.
- **Principal-right-ideal extension:** For arbitrary source and target rings
  `R` and `S` and a unital map `f : R →+* S`, the
  [principal-ideal interface](ProjectiveModules/Module/RightExtensionIdeal.lean)
  sends `m ⊗ s` to `op (f (unop m) * s)`. If the generator `e` maps to one,
  this is an equivalence with the regular right target module, with inverse
  `op s ↦ e ⊗ s`; no idempotence assumption on `e` is needed. If `e` is
  idempotent and maps to zero, the entire extension is zero. These two
  computations retain distinct hypotheses and require neither commutative
  nor nonzero scalars. An idempotent principal right ideal and its scalar
  extension are bundled as finite projectives over arbitrary rings.
- **Exterior constructions:** The [full exterior algebra](ProjectiveModules/ExteriorAlgebra/BaseChange.lean)
  commutes with arbitrary scalar extension; separately, the
  [fixed-degree binary Sum Formula](ProjectiveModules/ExteriorPower/DirectSum.lean)
  decomposes an exterior power of a direct sum into complementary-degree
  tensor products and is natural for module maps over the fixed ring. Neither
  supplies a fixed-degree scalar-extension theorem. The library also proves
  [projectivity of exterior powers](ProjectiveModules/ExteriorPower/Projective.lean)
  and constructs [determinants](ProjectiveModules/ExteriorPower/Determinant.lean).
- **Duality and invertibility:** The [finite-projective dual](ProjectiveModules/Dual/BaseChange.lean)
  and its [evaluation pairing](ProjectiveModules/Contraction/BaseChange.lean)
  commute with base change. The
  [rank-one characterization of invertibles](ProjectiveModules/Invertible/OfRankOne.lean)
  includes zero rings; [endomorphisms](ProjectiveModules/Invertible/Endomorphism.lean)
  are scalars. Only a [common finite free summand](ProjectiveModules/Invertible/StableCancellation.lean)
  is cancelled from two invertibles.
- **Ordinary-coefficient bidual evaluation:** For a possibly noncommutative
  semiring `R` and a finitely generated projective left `R`-module `M` whose
  addition is a commutative monoid,
  [`LinearMap.bidualEvalEquiv R M`](ProjectiveModules/Module/Bidual.lean) gives
  `M ≃ₗ[R] ((M →ₗ[R] R) →ₗ[Rᵐᵒᵖ] R)`. Its forward map is `LinearMap.flip`
  of the identity, sending `m` to `f ↦ f m`. The ordinary coefficient `R`
  has its canonical regular actions: the first Hom is an `Rᵐᵒᵖ`-module by
  `(op a • f)(m) = f(m) * a`, and the second a left `R`-module by
  `(a • Φ)(f) = a * Φ(f)`. Scalar and module universes are independent;
  no right action on `M`, additive inverses or freeness is assumed. The API
  supplies application, underlying-map, inverse evaluation and naturality laws.
  Over commutative semirings, two inverse inner-scalar restrictions compare
  it with Mathlib's `Module.evalEquiv`, preserving coefficient `R` and outer
  scalars `R`.
- **Finite-projective endomorphism trace:** For a commutative semiring `R` and
  a finitely generated projective `R`-module `M` with additive commutative
  monoid structure,
  [`LinearMap.traceOfFiniteProjective R M`](ProjectiveModules/Module/Trace.lean)
  is the linear contraction trace, without a basis or additive inverses.
  It evaluates a rank-one map `φ.smulRight x` to `φ x`, is invariant under
  linear equivalences across independent carrier universes, and agrees with
  Mathlib's finite-basis trace on finite free modules. This agreement is not
  asserted on every nonfree projective: the identity of the ideal generated
  by `(1, 0)` over `ℕ × ℕ` has contraction trace `(1, 0)`, which is nonzero
  and is not a natural multiple of the scalar unit.
- **Right-module categorical dual:** For a fixed arbitrary ring `R`,
  [`ModuleCat.rightDual`](ProjectiveModules/Dual/ModuleCat.lean) is the
  contravariant functor from right `R`-modules to left `R`-modules, with ordinary
  carrier `M →ₗ[Rᵐᵒᵖ] R`, left action `(r • f)(m) = r * f(m)` and maps given by
  precomposition. Scalar and module universes are independent; the output
  universe is their maximum.
- **Linear maps from finite projective modules:** For semirings `A` and `S`,
  let `M` be a finite projective `A`-module and let `N` carry commuting
  `A`- and `S`-module structures. The
  [linear-map closure theorems](ProjectiveModules/Module/FiniteProjectiveHom.lean)
  give two separate conclusions: `M →ₗ[A] N` is projective over `S` when
  `N` is projective over `S`, and finite over `S` when `N` is finite over `S`.
  Neither conclusion requires the other target property, an `S`-action on `M`,
  or additive groups. All scalar and carrier universes are independent.
- **Quotient Hom and restriction kernels:** For a ring `A`, a semiring `S`
  and additive-group `A`-modules `M` and `C`, suppose `C` also has an
  `S`-module structure commuting with its `A`-action. The
  [quotient-Hom equivalence](ProjectiveModules/Module/QuotientHom.lean)
  identifies `A`-linear maps from `M/P` to `C` with the kernel of restriction
  of forms to `P`, as an `S`-linear equivalence. No `S`-action on `M`,
  finite generation or projectivity is assumed. When `A = S = C` is a
  commutative ring, the [duality comparison](ProjectiveModules/Dual/Quotient.lean)
  identifies the entire equivalence with Mathlib's quotient-dual/annihilator
  equivalence; its forward and inverse linear maps are respectively the dual
  of the quotient map and the dual copairing.
- **Noncommutative local freeness:** For a proper two-sided ideal whose
  complement consists of units, every projective *right* module is free,
  without a rank or generation bound. This
  [arbitrary-rank endpoint](ProjectiveModules/Module/ArbitraryLocalProjectiveFree.lean)
  assembles projective countable-coordinate filtration increments that are
  free under the local hypotheses. The [split-range assembly](ProjectiveModules/Module/TransfiniteRangeBasis.lean)
  itself requires explicitly free increments and idempotence, not just an
  arbitrary idempotent range. Properness excludes the zero ring. The
  [standard local-ring scalar form](ProjectiveModules/Module/LocalRing.lean)
  states `Module.Projective R P → Module.Free R P` using `[Ring R]`
  and `[IsLocalRing R]`, with independent scalar and module universes and
  no finite or countable generation hypothesis. Commutativity is not required;
  right modules are recovered by specializing the scalars to `Rᵐᵒᵖ`.
- **Projectivity under scalar restriction:** Over semirings `R` and `S`,
  [`Module.Projective.trans`](ProjectiveModules/Module/ScalarRestriction.lean)
  makes any projective `S`-module `M` projective over `R` when `S` is
  projective over `R` and both scalar towers `R`–`S`–`S` and `R`–`S`–`M`
  hold for the given actions. No finite-generation assumption on `M` is needed.
  For arbitrary rings and a unital map `φ : R →+* S`,
  [`ModuleCat.FiniteProjective.restrictScalars`](ProjectiveModules/Category/FiniteProjectiveRestriction.lean)
  preserves finite projectives provided the canonical restricted left regular
  coefficient `(ModuleCat.restrictScalars φ).obj (ModuleCat.of S S)` is
  finite projective over `R`, with action `r • s = φ r * s`. Right modules
  use the same constructor at `φ.op`, requiring its own restricted opposite
  regular coefficient to be finite projective, with ordinary-element action
  `s * φ r`; the left coefficient hypothesis alone does not imply the right
  one. Neither commutativity nor a central image is assumed. The two scalar
  universes and the module carrier universe are independent, and restriction
  leaves the carrier universe unchanged.
- **Finite-projective size and representatives:** For an arbitrary ring
  `R : Type u` and independent carrier universe `v`,
  `ModuleCat.FiniteProjective.{u, v} R` is essentially small in universe `u`,
  without an assumed `Small` premise. The
  [representative interface](ProjectiveModules/Category/FiniteProjectiveSmallness.lean)
  places every finite projective input in `ModuleCat.FiniteProjective.{u, u} R`
  and supplies a linear equivalence to the original module, whose carrier
  universe is unrestricted. Its `isoClass` map uses the skeleton of that same
  reference category: two input classes agree exactly when the inputs are
  linearly equivalent, even across carrier universes. Applying the interface
  to `Rᵐᵒᵖ` gives right modules. Essential smallness alone does not make a
  category with a smaller carrier universe contain all finite projective
  classes.
- **Finite-free stabilization:** If `M × T` and `N × T` are linearly
  equivalent and `T` is finite projective, then `M` and `N` become equivalent
  after adjoining the same standard finite free module `Fin n → R`. The
  [common-stabilizer interface](ProjectiveModules/Module/FiniteStabilization.lean)
  works over arbitrary rings and independent carrier universes, without
  assuming that `M` or `N` is finite, free or projective. The witness `n` is
  existential, not a prescribed rank; no module cancellation or IBN is used.
- **Finite dimension bridges:** Finitely generated free objects in
  `ModuleCat` and `FGModuleCat` over a ring satisfying the strong rank
  condition are isomorphic exactly when their finite ranks agree. The
  [dimension interface](ProjectiveModules/Category/FiniteModuleDimension.lean)
  also preserves finite rank under the chosen ring-universe representative,
  with independent scalar and carrier universes. Over a division ring,
  freeness follows automatically. This is not rank classification of all
  finite projectives over arbitrary rings.
- **Right regular modules:** For every semiring, including the zero semiring,
  the ordinary carrier `R` is free and finite as an `Rᵐᵒᵖ`-module under the
  canonical action `op a • x = x * a`; projectivity follows from freeness.
  The [regular-module instances](ProjectiveModules/Module/RightRegular.lean)
  transport Mathlib's left regular module through its opposite equivalence.
- **Right-module classes and K₀:** Over any ring `R : Type u`, including the
  zero ring, the [class monoid and its group completion](ProjectiveModules/KTheory/K0.lean)
  inhabit `Type u`. Finite projective inputs may have independent carrier
  universes: ring-universe representatives give the complete reference
  category. Class equality means linear equivalence, whereas K₀ generator
  equality means [common stabilization](ProjectiveModules/KTheory/Stabilization.lean).
  Every K₀ element is a difference of generators. Generator equality and
  equality of differences have both finite-projective and standard finite-free
  stabilization criteria; differences use the cross-sums `P × Q'` and
  `P' × Q`. The free rank is existential, without IBN, nonzero scalars or
  module cancellation. Descent of isomorphism-invariant, zero-preserving,
  direct-sum-additive functions and the group-completion universal property
  come with evaluation and uniqueness laws.
- **Scalar extension on classes and K₀:** Every unital homomorphism between
  arbitrary rings induces additive maps on finite-projective right-module
  classes and K₀. The [change-of-rings interface](ProjectiveModules/KTheory/ScalarExtension.lean)
  evaluates on modules in independent carrier universes and respects identity
  homomorphisms and composition as equalities of entire additive maps.
  Noncommutative and zero rings are included; the induced maps need not be
  injective, and no projective-module cancellation is asserted. For a division-ring
  source and an arbitrary ring target, `ProjectiveClass.map_eq_map` and
  `K0.map_eq_map` identify the entire maps induced by any two scalar homomorphisms.
- **Dimension classification over division rings:** For `[DivisionRing D]`,
  [finite dimension](ProjectiveModules/KTheory/Rank.lean) gives additive equivalences
  `KTheory.ProjectiveClass.finrankEquiv D : ProjectiveClass D ≃+ ℕ` and
  `KTheory.K0.finrankEquiv D : K0 D ≃+ ℤ`, with the canonical `finrankHom` maps
  as their forward homomorphisms. The inverses send `n : ℕ` to the class of
  `Fin n → Dᵐᵒᵖ` and every `z : ℤ`, including negatives, to `z` times the
  regular right-module generator. Evaluation accepts finitely generated modules
  in independent carrier universes. Scalar extension along any unital map
  between division rings preserves both dimensions as equalities of entire
  additive homomorphisms. The induced class and K₀ maps are bijective by
  `ProjectiveClass.map_bijective` and `K0.map_bijective`, even if the scalar
  homomorphism is not surjective. Over a [zero ring](ProjectiveModules/KTheory/ZeroRing.lean),
  which is not a division ring, both the class monoid and the entire K₀ group
  are subsingletons; module dimension is not used as an additive invariant there.
- **Stable categories and rank:** [Stable module morphisms](ProjectiveModules/Category/Stable.lean)
  modulo maps through projectives form a *preadditive* category over a ring;
  the Ext¹ criterion additionally requires `Small.{uM} R`. For commutative
  rings, [locally constant stalk ranks](ProjectiveModules/Module/LocallyConstantRank.lean)
  are realized by finite projectives, and
  [rank fibers](ProjectiveModules/Module/RankFiberDecomposition.lean) decompose
  them. Classification by rank concerns the explicit
  [componentwise-free finite-projective class](ProjectiveModules/Module/ComponentwiseFree.lean),
  not every finite projective.
- **Free and finite linear-map modules:** Let `A` and `S` be semirings and
  `M` and `N` additive commutative monoids. Assume `M` is a finite free
  `A`-module and `N` has commuting `A`- and `S`-module actions
  (`SMulCommClass A S N`).
  [`Module.Free.linearMap_of_finite`](ProjectiveModules/Free/LinearMap.lean)
  makes `M →ₗ[A] N` free over `S` when `N` is free over `S`, without
  requiring `N` to be finite. Independently,
  [`Module.Finite.linearMap_of_projective`](ProjectiveModules/Module/FiniteProjectiveHom.lean)
  makes it finite over `S` when `N` is finite over `S`, without requiring
  `N` to be free. No `S`-action on `M`, scalar commutativity, nontriviality
  or rank condition is assumed; all four types may inhabit independent universes.
- **Bounded free-module endpoints:** A finite coordinate summand can be
  cancelled from a [countably free product](ProjectiveModules/Free/CountableCancellation.lean),
  and the countably free module
  [absorbs its direct summands](ProjectiveModules/Free/CountableAbsorption.lean).
  [Semisimple freeness](ProjectiveModules/Free/Semisimple.lean) requires a
  *finite stably-free* module. The
  [Laurent endpoint](ProjectiveModules/Module/LaurentPolynomial.lean)
  proves finite-projective freeness over the one-variable Laurent polynomial
  ring of a field, not multivariable Quillen–Suslin.
- **Finite direct summands:** A Dedekind-finite endomorphism monoid forces the
  complement in `M ≃ₗ[R] M × P` to be trivial
  ([generic cancellation](ProjectiveModules/Module/SummandCancellation.lean)).
  [Finite-free and finite-projective cancellation](ProjectiveModules/Free/FiniteFreeSummand.lean)
  specialize this to stably finite semirings and rings, respectively; a rank
  condition bounds the ranks of finite-free direct summands. Over rings,
  finite-coordinate product equivalences characterize both the rank condition
  and stable finiteness. The complementary module need not be finite or free.
- **Specified right inverses:** For any ring and `x * y = 1`, the right
  regular module is linearly equivalent to the product of itself and the
  right submodule generated by `1 - y * x`, with coordinates
  `(x * r, (1 - y * x) * r)` and inverse `(s, p) ↦ y * s + p`.
  [The defect interface](ProjectiveModules/Module/RightRegularDefect.lean)
  identifies that submodule with the kernel of left multiplication by `x`;
  it vanishes exactly when `y * x = 1`, without a premise on `x * y` for
  this vanishing criterion. No commutativity or nontriviality is assumed,
  and the complement is not asserted to be free or left-stable.
- **Infinite free coordinates:** Over a nontrivial semiring, two bases of the
  same module have equal lifted index cardinalities when the first index is
  infinite, without invariant basis number, a rank condition or commutative
  scalars. [The coordinate classification](ProjectiveModules/Free/InfiniteCoordinates.lean)
  characterizes linear equivalences of infinite free coordinate modules by
  equivalences of their indices, even across index universes, and excludes
  finite target indices. Its chosen index equivalence need not map basis
  vectors to basis vectors; finite-rank uniqueness is not asserted.
- **Fixed-simple multiplicity:** For a simple left module `S` over an arbitrary
  ring and a semisimple module isotypic of type `S`, the
  [multiplicity interface](ProjectiveModules/Module/IsotypicMultiplicity.lean)
  defines a cardinal using the rank of `Hom(S, M)` over the opposite division
  ring of `End(S)`. Its statements compare arbitrary direct-sum indices across
  universes, cover zero and one copy, and preserve multiplicity under a chosen
  ring equivalence with compatible semilinear equivalences of ambient modules
  and specified simple types, using independent universe lifts. Both types must
  be simple and both ambient modules semisimple and isotypic of their specified
  types. They do not assign a multiplicity of all of `M` when `M` is not
  semisimple and isotypic of the specified simple type.
- **Right matrix rows:** Over a division ring and a finite nonempty index type,
  the canonical opposite-matrix action on a row is simple. The
  [matrix-row interface](ProjectiveModules/Module/MatrixRow.lean) identifies
  every right module as isotypic of this row and supplies a direct-sum
  decomposition without a finite-generation or nonzero-module assumption.
  Its multiplicity is given by the fixed-simple interface above.
- **Scalar rank of fixed-simple modules:** For a scalar semiring satisfying
  the strong rank condition, a fixed simple type free over those scalars,
  and compatible scalar actions,
  the [scalar-rank interface](ProjectiveModules/Module/ScalarRank.lean) compares
  the scalar rank of a semisimple isotypic module with the rank of one simple
  summand times its cardinal multiplicity. Restriction along the opposite of
  `Matrix.scalar` identifies the canonical row action with componentwise
  right multiplication and gives the corresponding dimension identity for
  every right module over `Matrix ι ι D`, with `D` a division ring and `ι`
  finite and nonempty, including nonfree modules and infinite direct sums.
- **Finite stable presentations:** Over an arbitrary ring, a module admits a
  finite-coordinate stable presentation exactly when it is finitely generated
  and stably free; stable freeness alone also permits infinite free modules.
  [The finite stable presentation interface](ProjectiveModules/Free/StablePresentation.lean)
  compares any two presentations over an invariant-basis-number semiring by a
  cross-sum equality and a common *integer* coordinate difference, without
  assuming that the presented module is free, that the ring is commutative,
  or that a rank condition holds. This is not the generators-and-relations
  notion `Module.FinitePresentation`.
- **Stably free kernels:** A surjection from a stably free module onto a finite
  stably free module has stably free kernel. Finite stably free modules are
  precisely kernels of surjections between finite coordinate modules;
  [finite matrix kernels](ProjectiveModules/Free/StableKernel.lean) specialize
  this to right-linear maps over arbitrary rings, without an IBN hypothesis.
- **Chosen right-free coefficient transport:** Along any unital homomorphism
  `f : R →+* S` of possibly noncommutative semirings,
  [`Module.rightFreeLinearMapScalarExtension`](ProjectiveModules/Module/RightEndomorphismMatrix.lean)
  transports a supplied map `g : (ι → R) →ₗ[Rᵐᵒᵖ] (κ → R)` to a right-linear
  map over `S`. Only the source index `ι` must be finite; `κ` is arbitrary.
  At a target coordinate `i`, the result on `y : ι → S` is
  `∑ j, f (g (Pi.single j 1) i) * y j`: coefficients multiply on the left,
  while module scalars act on the right. With both index types finite,
  [`Module.rightFreeScalarExtensionEquiv`](ProjectiveModules/Module/RightEndomorphismMatrix.lean)
  transports a supplied equivalence and its inverse, with the same application
  formula; it does not assert the existence of an equivalence between unequal
  finite ranks. The homomorphism need not be injective or surjective; no
  commutativity, IBN or equality of index cardinalities is assumed. Scalar and
  index universes are independent. Characteristic and functorial laws cover
  basis vectors, coefficient images, zero/addition, identities and composition
  of maps and coefficient homomorphisms. Map composition additionally needs a
  finite intermediate index. Equivalence transport is compatible with underlying
  maps, inverses and composition. The Ring tensor bridges require both index
  types finite; their `_of_decidableEq` variants use caller equality-decision
  data, not extra hypotheses on the primary constructions.
- **Inner-conjugate right scalar extensions:** For arbitrary rings `R` and `S`,
  homomorphisms `φ ψ : R →+* S`, and a unit `u : Sˣ` satisfying
  `u * φ(r) = ψ(r) * u` for every `r`,
  [`ModuleCat.RightExtension.innerLinearEquiv`](ProjectiveModules/Module/RightExtensionConjugacy.lean)
  compares the two extensions of any right `R`-module by a right-`S`-linear
  equivalence. It sends `m ⊗_φ s` to `m ⊗_ψ (u * s)`; its inverse uses
  `u⁻¹`. The right target action stays fixed, including for noncentral units.
  One-generator values characterize the entire map, and the unit `1` gives
  the identity. Scalar and carrier universes are independent, with no
  commutativity, finiteness or projectivity hypothesis. Matrix, zero, empty
  and independently lifted examples distinguish the coefficient order.
- **Right scalar-extension comparisons and coherence:** For arbitrary rings
  `R`, `S`, `T`, a right `R`-module `M`, and unital homomorphisms
  `φ : R →+* S`, `ψ : S →+* T`,
  [`ModuleCat.RightExtension.idLinearEquiv` and `compLinearEquiv`](ProjectiveModules/Module/RightExtensionComparison.lean)
  compare identity and iterated extension with the original module and direct
  composite extension. Identity sends `m ⊗ r ↦ op r • m`, with inverse
  `m ↦ m ⊗ 1`; composition sends `(m ⊗ s) ⊗ t ↦ m ⊗ (ψ(s) * t)`, with
  inverse `m ⊗ t ↦ (m ⊗ 1) ⊗ t`. Scalar and module universes are independent;
  no finiteness or projectivity is required. Four characteristic `[simp]`
  equations describe both directions, with
  [noncommutative matrix, zero-boundary and independent-universe examples](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/RightExtensionComparisonClient.lean).
  The [unit and associativity laws](ProjectiveModules/Module/RightExtensionComparisonCoherence.lean)
  identify composition with an identity first with scalar extension of the
  identity comparison, and composition with an identity last with the identity
  comparison on the extended module. For a further ring map `θ : T →+* U`,
  the two triple-comparison routes agree as linear maps. On
  `((m ⊗ s) ⊗ t) ⊗ u`, this is the ordered coefficient identity
  `θ (ψ s * t) * u = θ (ψ s) * (θ t * u)`, without commutativity.
  All four scalar universes and the module universe are independent, and the
  right module needs only an additive commutative group and its opposite-ring
  action. [Matrix and raised-universe clients](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/RightExtensionComparisonCoherenceClient.lean)
  apply all three laws and detect both incorrect multiplication orders.
  These are pointwise linear-map or linear-equivalence equalities, not
  categorical natural isomorphisms or triangle and pentagon laws.
- **Commutative-base tensor comparison:** For a commutative ring `R`, an
  arbitrary ring `S`, a unital map `φ : R →+* S` and any right `R`-module
  `M`, the [module-first comparison](ProjectiveModules/Module/RightExtensionTensorProduct.lean)
  `ModuleCat.RightExtension.tensorProductLinearEquiv` identifies right scalar
  extension with Mathlib's `M ⊗[R] S`, using the opposite scalar view of `M`
  and the coefficient action `r • s = φ r * s`. Its right target action is
  `op t • (m ⊗ s) = m ⊗ (s * t)`, even when the image of `φ` is noncentral.
  The comparison and its inverse preserve pure tensors and intertwine module
  maps with Mathlib tensor maps, including the right-extension `functor'`.
  If `S` is also commutative, `extendScalarsLinearEquiv` identifies the same
  extension with the right-module view of Mathlib's scalar-first
  `ModuleCat.extendScalars φ`, sending `m ⊗ s` to `s ⊗ m`. The two
  comparisons agree via `TensorProduct.comm`; both directions and map
  naturality are characterized. Scalar universes and the module carrier
  universe are independent, with output carrier in `Type (max uM uS)`.
  Neither finiteness, projectivity, injectivity, nontriviality nor an
  `Algebra R S` structure is assumed. These are pointwise linear
  equivalences, not a categorical natural isomorphism or a coherence package.
- **Rank condition and invariant basis number:** Both properties pull back
  along any unital homomorphism of semirings, without injectivity or
  surjectivity. [The rank-condition pullback](ProjectiveModules/Free/InvariantBasisNumber.lean)
  assumes the target has rank condition; the invariant-basis-number pullback
  assumes only target invariant basis number, not rank condition. Source and
  target may inhabit independent universes.
- **Free-word generator independence:** For any semiring `K` and any alphabet
  `ι` in an independent universe, the letter monomials form a linearly independent
  family in the **left A-module** `A = MonoidAlgebra K (FreeMonoid ι)`.
  Canonical finitely supported linear combinations multiply polynomial coordinates
  by their letters on the **right**; coefficients at disjoint terminal letters
  recover every coordinate. This is independence over **A**, not just over `K`.
  Injective reindexing gives independent subfamilies, including infinite ones.
  The [suffix-family examples](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/FreeWordSuffixFamilyClient.lean)
  include rational, noncommutative matrix, infinite-index and trivial-coefficient
  cases and the failure of repeating a letter over nontrivial coefficients.
  For distinct letters `a` and `b`, the finite-coordinate equivalence identifies
  the canonical linear combination with the
  [word-map interface](ProjectiveModules/Free/FreeWordAlgebra.lean), which sends
  `(p, q)` to `p * single (of a) 1 + q * single (of b) 1` over
  `A = MonoidAlgebra K (FreeMonoid ι)`. It is **left A-linear** and appends
  letters on the **right**. It is injective: coefficients at disjoint final
  suffixes recover both inputs. Transporting its codomain to `Fin 1 → A` gives
  failure of the strong rank condition. No commutativity, nontriviality or
  finite-alphabet assumption is imposed; trivial coefficients give singleton
  modules. The
  [rational-Boolean client](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/FreeWordAlgebraClient.lean)
  separately obtains rank condition and invariant basis number from augmentation;
  neither property is asserted for every coefficient semiring. The free-algebra
  contrast follows Mathlib's discussion credited to T. Y. Lam.
- **Free-word augmentation kernel:** The
  [zero-generator augmentation](ProjectiveModules/Free/FreeWordAugmentation.lean)
  returns the coefficient of the empty word. Over any semiring `K` and any
  alphabet `α` in an independent universe, the
  [full-letter linear combination](ProjectiveModules/Free/FreeWordAugmentationKernel.lean)
  has range equal to the kernel of the augmentation's canonical semilinear
  map. Coordinates use the left regular action of the word semiring and
  append letters on the right. Noncommutative and trivial coefficients and
  empty or infinite alphabets are included; no basis or linear equivalence
  onto the kernel is constructed.
- **Cyclic additive traces and invariant basis number:** For an additive
  commutative monoid with multiplication, a cyclic additive map into any
  additive commutative monoid identifies the traces of opposite matrix
  products for finite (possibly empty) rectangular index types. Over a
  semiring, injectivity of the natural multiples of the image of one under
  such a map implies invariant basis number, without multiplication on the
  target or a rank condition on the source. See the
  [cyclic trace criteria](ProjectiveModules/Free/CyclicTrace.lean).

These are declarations of this library whose proofs reuse its pinned
[dependencies](#build); they are not presented as imported results or as
complete coverage of a motivating book.

## Use

Add the library to your `lakefile.toml`:

```toml
[[require]]
name = "projective-modules"
git = "https://github.com/FormalFrontier/projective-modules.git"
rev = "main"
```

GitHub `main` contains reviewed releases. Lake resolves the latest release when
this dependency is first added or updated; `lake-manifest.json` locks the resolved
commit until the next update. To pin a particular release, replace `main` with a
full commit from that history.

Lake resolves one Mathlib revision for an entire project. When combining
libraries, choose compatible releases and retain the matching toolchain and
dependency lock file. The [build section](#build) describes this checkout's
resolved dependency graph.

Use `import ProjectiveModules` for the production aggregate, or import a
focused module from the [module map](#module-map). No source-research
repository is needed to use the library.

```lean
import ProjectiveModules

example (R M : Type*) [CommRing R] [AddCommGroup M] [Module R M] :
    Module.Invertible R M ↔
      Module.Finite R M ∧ Module.Projective R M ∧
        Module.rankAtStalk (R := R) M = 1 :=
  Module.invertible_iff_finite_projective_rankAtStalk_eq_one

example (R P : Type*) [Ring R] [AddCommGroup P] [Module R P]
    (n : ℕ) (e : (P × (Fin n →₀ R)) ≃ₗ[R] (ℕ →₀ R)) :
    Nonempty (P ≃ₗ[R] (ℕ →₀ R)) :=
  ⟨Module.Free.natFinsuppEquivOfProdFinFinsuppEquiv R n e⟩
```

For the Noetherian theorem alone, use
`import ProjectiveModules.Module.CountablyGenerated.Noetherian`; the
[guide](docs/NoetherianSubmodule.md) gives exact hypotheses and an integer
finsupp example. Examples and ordinary aggregate-import regression clients
belong to a separate default build target, not the production aggregate.

## Mathematical scope

The [linear-map interface](ProjectiveModules/Free/LinearMap.lean) uses Mathlib's
existing `(Module.Free.chooseBasis A M).constr S` universal-property
equivalence, allowing independent scalar and module universes.
`Module.Free.constr_symm_eq_repr_congrLeft_lsum` identifies its inverse with
the composition of the chosen basis's `repr.congrLeft N S`,
`(Finsupp.lsum S).symm` and the pointwise equivalences
`LinearMap.ringLmapEquivSelf A S N`. This equality needs `M` free over `A`
but not finite, and does not require `N` to be free or finite. The existing
`Module.Free.constr` is definitionally the same producer when its outer-scalar
and target-module universe constraints fit.

The [zero-generator augmentation](ProjectiveModules/Free/FreeWordAugmentation.lean)
`ε₀ = MonoidAlgebra.freeMonoidAugmentation K α` maps the free-word semiring
`A = MonoidAlgebra K (FreeMonoid α)` to `K`, fixes constant monomials and sends
letter monomials to zero. Its monomial formula uses the universal word map
sending every letter to zero, and `freeMonoidAugmentation_apply` gives
`ε₀ x = x.coeff 1`, the coefficient of the empty word. The
[kernel interface](ProjectiveModules/Free/FreeWordAugmentationKernel.lean) identifies
`LinearMap.range (Finsupp.linearCombination A (fun a ↦ single (FreeMonoid.of a) 1))`
with `LinearMap.ker ε₀.toSemilinearMap`. Both are submodules of the canonical
left regular `A`-module: polynomial coordinates multiply letter monomials on
the right. The semilinear map uses the canonical self-actions on `A` and `K`,
not a global `A`-module instance on `K`.

Coefficients may form any semiring, including noncommutative and trivial
semirings; coefficient and alphabet universes are independent, and empty or
infinite alphabets are allowed. This augmentation differs from the
coefficient-sum augmentation when the coefficients are nontrivial and a letter
is present; the two maps agree for empty alphabets or trivial coefficients.
The kernel interface constructs neither a basis nor a linear equivalence
onto the kernel.

Right modules use opposite-ring actions where needed: the
[balanced tensor](ProjectiveModules/Module/BalancedTensorProduct.lean) and
[right extension](ProjectiveModules/Module/RightExtension.lean) preserve
noncommutative multiplication order. Extension/restriction, quotient and
finite free-coordinate equivalences do not assume commutativity. Tensoring
with a projective right module preserves the three steps of a short exact
sequence of left modules; [Hom exactness](ProjectiveModules/Module/HomExact.lean)
over semirings is additive rather than an unwarranted scalar-linear statement.
[Projective complements](ProjectiveModules/Module/ProjectiveComplement.lean)
split surjections and relate finite spanning families to free summands.

The [finite-projective restriction examples](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/FiniteProjectiveRestrictionFunctorClient.lean)
use the nonzero, nonfree principal right ideal of a noncentral diagonal
idempotent in `M₂(ℤ)`, with independently lifted scalar rings and ideal carrier.
The restricted doubling map moves a nonzero generator to a nonzero value;
right multiplication by the idempotent kills a nonzero upper-right member,
whereas the reversed coefficient product is nonzero. The regular coefficient
in these examples is finite and free, not a nonfree coefficient example.
These restriction interfaces do not supply a direct-sum-additivity interface
or restriction maps on projective classes or K₀.

Over a proper two-sided ideal with unit complement, the finite and countable
right-module freeness steps reduce to a quotient division ring; the
arbitrary-rank step uses countable invariant supports of an idempotent on an
arbitrarily indexed free module. A supported range increment is projective and,
for a countable coordinate difference, countably generated; the global range
is free when its successive split increments are free. The endpoint retains
the proper-ideal/unit hypotheses, while the filtration theorem alone does
not assert that every idempotent range is free.

For commutative rings, locally constant natural-valued rank functions on the
prime spectrum are realized by finite projectives and complements. A finite
projective decomposes over finitely many nonempty rank fibers via clopen
idempotents. A componentwise-free module has an explicit finite-free model on
a complete orthogonal idempotent decomposition and is classified within that
class by stalk rank. Zero modules and empty index types remain valid where the
individual declarations permit them. `Module.componentwiseFreeModel_finite`
and `Module.componentwiseFreeModel_projective` use `[Finite I]`, with
idempotence still required by the projectivity theorem; explicit external
instance-argument compatibility with their earlier `[Fintype I]` versions is
not asserted.

For any semiring, `Ideal.span {e}` is the canonical **left** ideal, and its
linear projection is **right** multiplication `r ↦ r * e`. Idempotence makes
this map a retraction of inclusion and gives projectivity. Over any ring the
complementary left ideals form a product linearly equivalent to the regular
left module: the forward map is addition and the inverse is
`r ↦ (r * e, r * (1 - e))`. No two-sided-ideal or centrality premise is used.
The [matrix clients](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/IdempotentSpanSemiringClient.lean)
use canonical `M₂(ℕ)` and `M₂(ℤ)` operations. Their matrix units illustrate
that left multiplication by a noncentral idempotent need not fix its left
ideal, whereas the right-multiplication projection does. The identity of
`M₂(ℕ)` has no additive inverse under its canonical addition.

For a commutative semiring, an idempotent `e` acts as the identity on
`Ideal.span {e}`. The ideal is free exactly for `e = 0` or `e = 1`; when
`e ≠ 0`, its nontriviality follows from its generator, and Mathlib's faithful
scalar action on a nontrivial free module forces `e = 1`. Over a commutative
ring, `1 - e` annihilates this ideal. The
[idempotent ideal clients](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/IdempotentSpanClient.lean)
exhibit the nonfree finite-projective ideal generated by `op (1, 0)` over
the opposite of a product of two fields in independent universes, as well as
zero/unit boundaries and a nonfree ideal over `ℕ × ℕ`. The freeness criterion
is not asserted for noncommutative scalars.

For right extension along an arbitrary ring map `f : R →+* S`, the principal
ideal generated by `op e` in `Rᵐᵒᵖ` evaluates in the regular right target
module by `m ⊗ s ↦ op (f (unop m) * s)`. The image-one equivalence has
characteristic equations for both directions; idempotence is required only
for the finite-projective constructors and the image-zero computation.
The constructors also allow arbitrary source and target rings. The source
bundle's identity linear equivalence to its principal ideal has equations
for both directions and identifies zero and scalar action through its map
laws. The
[integer matrix clients](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/RightIdealRingConstructorClient.lean)
bundle the right ideal of a noncentral diagonal idempotent in `M₂(ℤ)` and
its identity scalar extension. Their comparison sends `E₀₀ ⊗ E₀₁` to the
nonzero upper-right unit, whereas right multiplication of that unit by
`E₀₀` is zero.
[Projection clients](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/RightIdempotentExtensionClient.lean)
for independent-universe fields `K` and `L` give dimension pairs `(1, 0)` and
`(0, 1)` for the two principal right summands of `K × L`. Reduction of the
non-idempotent integer `3` modulo two and a zero target ring illustrate the
image-one hypothesis. Noncommutative matrix-product projections also give
an image-one equivalence for the non-idempotent generator `(E₀₁, 1)` and an
image-zero extension for the noncentral idempotent `(E₀₀, 0)`. Their pure-tensor
and inverse-map equations retain multiplication order. An explicit member
of the latter ideal is not fixed by the idempotent action. These are
scalar-extension computations of modules, not assertions that their K₀
generators are distinct.

The direct-sum monoid `KTheory.ProjectiveClass R` is a ring-universe resizing
of isomorphism classes in the complete finite-projective right-module reference
category. Its generators respect zero modules, products, linear equivalences,
representatives and carrier lifts. `KTheory.K0 R` is Mathlib's additive
Grothendieck group of that monoid, not a quotient that assumes projective-module
cancellation. The [common-stabilization clients](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/K0FiniteStabilizationClient.lean)
include explicitly nonfree finite projective ideals over `ℚ × ℚ`, products and
independent carrier lifts, and zero rings. Nonfreeness alone does not detect
nonzero K₀ classes. For arbitrary rings, finite coordinate classes are natural
multiples of the regular class by the direct-sum law, not an IBN assertion.

Over a division ring `D`, dimension descends by the monoid and group universal
properties and classifies the projective class monoid by ℕ and K₀ by ℤ.
`ProjectiveClass.finrankEquiv D` and `K0.finrankEquiv D` are `AddEquiv`s, not ring
equivalences; their whole forward homomorphisms are the existing canonical
`finrankHom` maps. The natural-number inverse is the class of `Fin n → Dᵐᵒᵖ`;
the integer inverse is `z • K0.of D Dᵐᵒᵖ` for every integer, including negatives.
The inverse and canonical dimension maps satisfy both round-trip laws and agree
with the canonical cast from ℕ to ℤ on class generators. Every finitely generated
right-module class and K₀ generator, in any carrier universe, is recovered from
its dimension; over a division ring, freeness and projectivity are automatic.
Equality of two such K₀ generators is equivalent to right-linear isomorphism,
even for independent carrier universes. The whole-homomorphism laws
`ProjectiveClass.finrankHom_comp_map` and `K0.finrankHom_comp_map` state that
scalar extension along any unital `φ : D →+* S` between division rings preserves
natural and integer dimensions. These classifications do not identify stable
K₀ equality with module isomorphism over arbitrary rings.

For a unital homomorphism between division rings, `ProjectiveClass.map_bijective`
and `K0.map_bijective` allow the induced maps to be bundled using
`AddEquiv.ofBijective`. This does not require the scalar homomorphism to be
surjective: the inclusion ℚ → ℂ is a proper extension with bijective class and
K₀ maps. With a division-ring source and any ring target, the whole-homomorphism
laws `ProjectiveClass.map_eq_map` and `K0.map_eq_map` show independence of the
scalar homomorphism. A finite source basis compares both extensions with the
same finite free target module; no target dimension invariant is needed.
The [scalar-map clients](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/K0RankClient.lean) include
identity versus conjugation on ℂ, rational-to-complex extension, lifted real
quaternions, collapse to a zero ring, and the distinct scalar embedding and
conjugate-composite embedding into the noncommutative ring `Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℂ`.
They compute every finite coordinate class and signed regular K₀ multiple,
including −3. Neither bijectivity nor independence of the induced maps asserts
full faithfulness, equivalence of the module categories, or equality of the
scalar-extension functors.

For the commutative-base tensor comparison, the source left action is
restriction of the given right module along `(RingEquiv.toOpposite R).toRingHom`;
the coefficient is `(ModuleCat.restrictScalars φ).obj (ModuleCat.of S S)`.
These actions are specified data, not an arbitrary `Module R S` on the same
carrier: for `R = S` and a nonidentity `φ`, the coefficient action need not
be the regular one. Its left `R`-action commutes with coefficient right
multiplication by associativity, without requiring a central image.
Module-first naturality uses the restricted source morphism tensored with the
identity of this coefficient module. With commutative `S`, scalar-first
naturality uses Mathlib's extension and restriction functor maps.
The [tensor clients](tests/ProjectiveModulesTests/RightExtensionTensorProductClient.lean)
include noncentral diagonal matrix coefficients, a swapped endomorphism of
`ℤ × ℤ`, zero boundaries and independently raised carrier/scalar universes.
Their public `modFive_tensor_unit_ne_zero` proves that the rank-one tensor
with coefficient one survives the noninjective map `ℤ → ZMod 5`, using a
balanced evaluation rather than the comparison equivalences.

## Documentation limits

The [API index](docs/README.md) links the [generated API](docs/API.md),
which covers a historical 43-module subset, not an exhaustive catalogue of the
current library. The Noetherian and infinite-coordinate modules are outside
that subset. Use the current modules, the focused
[Noetherian guide](docs/NoetherianSubmodule.md) and the
[infinite-coordinate module](ProjectiveModules/Free/InfiniteCoordinates.lean)
for those interfaces.

The full exterior-algebra base-change interface is not fixed-degree scalar
extension. Over arbitrary rings, rank does not classify all finite projectives.
The finite-projective size and isomorphism-class interfaces alone do not
construct a K₀ group. The separate K₀ modules form and use their direct-sum
class monoid and group completion; their arbitrary-ring stabilization criteria
do not assert cancellation of projectives or classification by dimension.
Division-ring dimension separately gives additive class and K₀ equivalences
with ℕ and ℤ. Right-extension comparison coherence gives
pointwise unit and triple-composition equalities, not categorical triangle or
pentagon laws, common-universe carrier lifts, or compatibility of scalar
extension with such lifts.
The stable category is preadditive, not abelian; its Ext¹ test needs
`Small.{uM} R`. Countable and finite-summand cancellation are bounded, not
arbitrary cancellation. Semisimple freeness still requires finite stably-free
modules. The Laurent result concerns one variable over a field. A source
citation does not assert a source-by-source correspondence or whole-book
formalization; such accounting belongs in source repositories.
